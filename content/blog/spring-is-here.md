Spring brings a lot of energy with it. Days get longer, people feel lighter, and there's a sense that anything is possible. Most of us have felt that same lift at the start of a new year, a new role, or a new project.

The challenge is that the feeling fades. By midsummer, the burst of enthusiasm has worn off and the team is back to business as usual. Great leaders don't wait for the next season to bring the energy back. They build it into how they lead every day.

## Your team takes its cue from you

People watch their leaders closely. If you show up with energy and purpose, your team notices. If you show up tired, distracted, or negative, they notice that too. Attitude isn't everything, but it sets the tone for everything else.

That doesn't mean faking enthusiasm. It means taking ownership of the attitude you bring through the door, especially on the hard days.

## Turn a good feeling into good habits

Energy lasts when it's attached to something concrete. A few ways to make it stick:

- **Set a few clear goals for the season.** Give your team something specific to rally around, and revisit those goals often.
- **Recognize progress, not just results.** Small wins keep momentum going between the big ones.
- **Start the day with a short huddle.** A few minutes of alignment each morning keeps everyone pointed in the same direction. (Here's [how to run an effective daily huddle](/blog/start-smart-utilizing-the-daily-huddle).)
- **Invest in relationships.** Take time to talk with your people one-to-one about their work and their goals.

## Results depend on relationships

In more than three decades of working with leaders, I've seen the same thing again and again: the strongest results come from the strongest relationships. When people trust their leader and feel connected to their team, they bring their best effort, and that effort doesn't fade with the season.

Use the fresh start in front of you. Then make it last.
