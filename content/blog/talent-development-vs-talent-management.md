**Talent management is the set of processes an organization uses to hire, evaluate, and retain people. Talent development is the deliberate work of growing people's skills so they can take on bigger roles.** Most organizations invest heavily in managing talent and far less in developing it. The ones that build a strong leadership bench from within do both.

## Talent management: filling and keeping roles

Talent management typically includes recruiting, onboarding, performance reviews, compensation, and retention programs. It answers the question: do we have the right people in the right roles today?

These processes matter. But on their own, they don't prepare people for what's next.

## Talent development: growing future leaders

Talent development focuses on building capability. It answers a different question: will we have the leaders we need tomorrow?

Effective talent development includes:

- Identifying high-potential employees early
- Giving them stretch assignments with support
- Coaching and mentoring on the job
- Building people skills, not just technical skills
- Preparing successors for key roles before they're needed

## Why classroom learning isn't enough

Many new leaders today are highly educated, and that should be commended. But much of what makes someone a strong leader can't be learned in a classroom: motivating a team through a hard week, handling a conflict between two good employees, or making a judgment call with incomplete information.

Those skills develop through experience, with the right guidance at the right time. That's why we focus on [development that happens in real work](/blog/education-vs-training-whats-more-important), not just in a training room.

## How to build your leadership bench

### 1. Identify your critical roles

Which positions would hurt most if they were vacant tomorrow? Start your development efforts there.

### 2. Spot potential, not just performance

Your best individual performer isn't always your best future leader. Look for people who earn trust, help others, take ownership, and want to grow.

### 3. Create development plans

For each high-potential employee, define the skills they need for their next role and the experiences that will build them.

### 4. Pair experience with coaching

Stretch assignments work best with support. Regular coaching conversations help people reflect on what they're learning and apply it.

### 5. Develop people skills deliberately

Cost control, productivity, and efficiency matter, but the soft skills of leading people are usually what determine whether a new leader succeeds.

### 6. Review progress regularly

Talent development is ongoing. Review your bench at least twice a year and adjust plans as people grow and needs change.

## The business case for developing from within

Promoting from within preserves knowledge of your operation and culture, signals to employees that there's a future for them, and often costs less than hiring from outside. It also improves retention, because people tend to stay where they see a path forward.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between talent development and training?

Training typically teaches specific knowledge or skills, often in a classroom. Talent development is broader and longer term, combining experience, coaching, and learning to prepare people for bigger roles.

### How do you identify high-potential employees?

Look beyond current performance to qualities like learning agility, ownership, the ability to influence others, and genuine interest in leading. Input from multiple leaders gives a more accurate picture.

### What are examples of talent development?

Examples include mentoring, leadership coaching, job rotations, stretch assignments, leadership trainee programs, and succession planning.

### How does talent development improve retention?

Employees are more likely to stay when they see a future with the organization and feel invested in. Development opportunities are among the strongest reasons people give for staying.
