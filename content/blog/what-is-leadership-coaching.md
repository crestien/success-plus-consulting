**Leadership coaching is a hands-on development partnership that helps a leader change how they lead people, not just what they know about leadership.** A coach works alongside the leader in their real role, helping them set clear expectations, hold people accountable, communicate under pressure, and build a team that hits its numbers. Done well, the results show up in behavior and business metrics, not in a certificate.

## Why so many leaders need coaching

Most supervisors and managers were promoted because they were excellent at the work. Then, almost overnight, they were asked to lead the people who do it. Very few were taught how.

The result is predictable. New leaders avoid hard conversations, or handle them badly. They do the work themselves instead of developing their team. They set goals without a plan to reach them. And the people they lead feel it, in morale, in engagement, and eventually in turnover.

In more than 30 years of working with organizations, I've found that the number one reason leaders struggle is that they step into the role without a plan for how they will lead.

## What leadership coaching covers

Every coaching engagement should be built around the leader and the organization, but most address a core set of questions:

- **Expectations:** How will you set clear, measurable expectations for your team?
- **Accountability:** How will you follow up, and what happens when expectations aren't met?
- **Motivation:** How will you keep people engaged when the work is hard or repetitive?
- **Communication:** How will you give feedback, recognize good work, and handle conflict?
- **Results:** Which KPIs matter most, and how does your leadership move them?

## Leadership coaching vs. leadership training

Training typically happens in a classroom. It shares concepts and frameworks with a group, and then everyone goes back to work. Some of it sticks. Much of it doesn't.

Coaching happens in the work itself. A coach observes, asks questions, helps the leader practice new approaches in real situations, and follows up on what happened. As one of our longtime clients put it, the Success Model "is not a classroom instrumental environment. It is relationship oriented through on-the-job interaction and mentoring. Think of it as coaching rather than training."

Both have a place. But when the goal is lasting behavior change, coaching is usually what makes the difference.

## Signs your leaders could benefit from coaching

- Turnover or absenteeism is higher on some teams than others
- Supervisors are strong technically but struggle to manage people
- The same performance problems keep coming back
- Leaders avoid difficult conversations or handle them inconsistently
- You're promoting from within and want new leaders to succeed faster
- An executive is taking on a larger role and needs a candid sounding board

## What results to expect

Good coaching should be measured in two ways: what the leader does differently, and what that does for the business. Look for clearer expectations and follow-through, stronger relationships between leaders and employees, fewer repeat issues, and improvement in the metrics that matter, such as productivity, quality, safety, and retention.

One client, an asset leader in manufacturing, told us his work teams improved in every major metric, some by more than 70 percent. More importantly, the improvements lasted.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does leadership coaching take?

It depends on the leader and the goals. Most engagements run several months, with regular coaching conversations and on-the-job observation. Lasting behavior change takes repetition, so we plan for follow-up rather than a single session.

### Who should get leadership coaching?

Coaching helps leaders at every level: new frontline supervisors, experienced managers taking on larger teams, and executives leading through growth or change. It's especially valuable for leaders who were promoted for technical skill.

### Is leadership coaching worth the investment?

When it's tied to business outcomes, yes. The cost of a struggling leader shows up in turnover, lost productivity, quality issues, and missed goals. Coaching that improves how a leader manages people typically pays for itself in those areas.

### What's the difference between a leadership coach and a mentor?

A coach focuses on helping a leader build specific skills and habits toward defined goals. A mentor offers broader guidance and perspective drawn from experience. Many leaders benefit from both. See our guide to [executive coaching vs. mentoring](/blog/executive-coach-vs-mentor).
