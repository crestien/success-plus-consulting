Every day offers a fresh start. Yesterday may not have gone the way you hoped. Last week may have been a hard one. But the sun rose again this morning, and it rose for everyone.

## A lesson I've never forgotten

Years ago, I had a conversation with Mrs. Ressler, the owner of Mrs. Ressler's Food Products. She was a Holocaust survivor, and her story left a permanent impression on me. One thing she said has stayed with me ever since:

> "I don't forget about the past, but I don't live in the past."

She had every reason to be defined by what happened to her. Instead, she chose to build something, to lead people, and to move forward. That's a powerful example for any leader.

## Learn from the past, then let it go

Good leaders take the past seriously. They study what went wrong, find the root cause, and make changes so it doesn't happen again. What they don't do is carry yesterday's frustrations into today's conversations.

When a leader holds onto a mistake, the team feels it. People become cautious, stop taking initiative, and wait to be told what to do. When a leader treats each day as a new opportunity, people are more willing to try again and do better.

## Give your people a fresh start too

This applies to the people you lead as much as it applies to you:

- **Address issues directly, then move on.** Once a problem has been discussed and a plan is in place, don't keep bringing it up.
- **Look for improvement.** When someone turns things around, notice it and say so.
- **Reset the team.** After a tough stretch, take a moment to acknowledge it, share what you've learned, and set the focus for what comes next.

The sun rose today for everyone, and it brings with it everything that today can offer. Are you living in the past, or leading your people into the future?
