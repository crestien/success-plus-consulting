In a [Harvard Business Review survey](https://hbr.org/2015/06/the-top-complaints-from-employees-about-their-leaders), employees were asked to name their top complaints about the leaders in their organizations.

The number one answer? **Not recognizing employee achievements.**

It's a striking result, because recognition costs almost nothing. Yet it's one of the most common gaps I see in leaders at every level, from frontline supervisors to the executive team.

## Why recognition matters so much

People genuinely want to do good work, and they want to know that their work matters. When a leader notices, it confirms both. When a leader doesn't, people start to wonder why they're putting in the extra effort.

Over time, a lack of recognition turns into lower engagement, less initiative, and eventually turnover. Withholding appreciation has an effect just as powerful as giving it, only in the opposite direction.

## Why leaders miss it

Most leaders don't intend to overlook their people. They're busy. They assume people know they're appreciated. Or they only speak up when something goes wrong, so the only feedback employees hear is criticism.

## How to recognize people well

- **Be specific.** "Great job" is nice. "The way you handled that customer issue saved us a big account" is meaningful.
- **Be timely.** Recognition means the most when it's close to the moment.
- **Connect it to the bigger picture.** Show people how their work contributes to the team's goals and the organization's success.
- **Make it a habit.** Build recognition into daily huddles and [one-to-one conversations](/blog/secrets-to-an-effective-one-to-one) so it doesn't depend on remembering.
- **Know your people.** Some appreciate public praise; others prefer a quiet word. Adapt to the person.

## A small habit with a big payoff

As a leader, your attention carries weight. When your people score a win, show appreciation, reinforce why their work matters, and encourage them to keep growing. That small habit will go a long way toward [keeping your employees motivated](/blog/5-ways-to-motivate-your-employees), and it's one of the simplest ways to become the kind of leader people want to work for.
